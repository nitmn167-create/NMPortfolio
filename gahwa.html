01 / THE BRIEF

A world stage for a living tradition.

What is the event?

The source brief presents an international championship for Emirati gahwa, planned for Abu Dhabi in March 2027. It brings the skill of preparing and serving gahwa into a competition setting.

What the identity needed to do

The design needed to respect the culture while giving the championship its own energy. It had to work across a logo, print, event spaces and motion.

The relationship

DCT Abu Dhabi is named as the owner in the brief, with Bait Al Gahwa as the cultural authority.

02 / MY CONTRIBUTION

My role: one idea across every touchpoint.

Concept and graphic design

I developed the visual concept, logo direction, colour theme and typography approach. I built the graphic language and applied it to the branding mock-ups.

Motion design

I translated the visual idea into a logo animation using drawings of coffee tools and serving gestures. The final sequence connects the ritual to the championship mark.

Presentation

I brought the research, rationale, logo system and applications into one presentation to explain how the identity works.

03 / RESEARCH

Start with the ritual.

What I looked at

I focused on the cup, the dallah, the pour and the gestures of serving. These everyday details gave the identity a direct link to gahwa.

Learn from the parent brand

Bait Al Gahwa describes the ceremony through drawn gestures, repeated lines and patterns. I used that visual logic as a starting point for the championship.

The design question

How could the careful movements of the ritual become a clear, recognisable event identity?

04 / BRAND CONNECTION

Keep the roots. Add event energy.

Shared visual language

Fine lines, fluid movement, repetition and precise spacing connect the championship to Bait Al Gahwa.

A distinct event identity

The championship has its own mark and event world. The proposed brand architecture keeps a visible relationship with the parent rather than treating it as a separate cultural story.

Why it matters

This approach gives the event room to feel ambitious and contemporary while staying connected to the practice it celebrates.

The original deck proposes parent-brand endorsement; it is a design direction, not a verified final rollout.

05 / LOGO CONCEPT

The cup. The pour. The rhythm of three.

The cup

A cup rim appears as a tilted ellipse when viewed from an angle. Stacked cups repeat that shape, giving me a simple form to build around.

The pour

The slanted stream of coffee introduces direction and movement. It makes the identity feel active even in a still layout.

Three repeated forms

The proposal uses three ellipses to express stages and rhythm in the ceremony, and progression through the championship.

06 / SKETCHES & DEVELOPMENT

From observed gesture to graphic shape.

Simplify the reference

The presentation connects the cup rim and the parent brand's gesture drawings to three overlapping ellipses. The repeated shape becomes the structure around the lettering.

Keep the motion visible

The tilted forms suggest the movement of serving. Fine outlines keep the construction light, while overlap gives the mark a sense of rhythm.

What is shown here

These are the concept diagrams and reference sketches included in the proposal. They explain the visual logic behind the mark.

07 / LETTERING & TYPOGRAPHY

A familiar voice, with a new rhythm.

Arabic logo direction

The rationale references Ruq'ah, a familiar Arabic writing style. Its changing stroke weight gives the logo a lively, expressive character.

English logo descriptor

The uppercase serif descriptor gives the English event name a formal supporting role. It sits below the Arabic mark without competing with it.

Presentation type

The supplied PDF uses Catamaran for its main English presentation text and includes Amiri and Arimo. These are embedded document fonts; the exact English logo typeface is not specified.

Ruq'ah is the stated lettering inspiration, not a confirmed downloadable font name for the logo.

08 / COLOUR SYSTEM

Colours that feel warm and grounded.

09 / LOGO SYSTEM

One mark, several useful formats.

Horizontal and vertical

I developed wide and stacked arrangements so the identity could fit different spaces, from a banner to a narrow hanging sign.

Outline and solid

The outline version feels light and detailed. The solid red version creates a stronger block of colour for busy or distant settings.

Small-size use

The proposal sets a minimum width of 45 mm in print and 240 px on screen for horizontal and vertical lock-ups. This protects the lettering at smaller sizes.

10 / KEY VISUAL

The logo becomes a wider visual language.

Repeat the same idea

I extended the tilted ellipse and dotted line into larger graphics. Repetition gives the layouts a rhythm that connects back to the ceremony.

Bring people and practice into the design

Photography of preparation and serving keeps the identity connected to real tools, hands and gestures.

Build clear layers

Photography tells the story, colour sets the mood, and the logo identifies the event. Keeping these roles clear helps the layouts stay consistent.

11 / OUTDOOR HOARDING

Make the brand visible at street scale.

What I designed

A large-format composition combining photography, red colour panels, the logo and supporting line graphics.

Why this layout

The large images introduce the cultural story quickly. Strong colour blocks separate the messages and help the identity remain visible across a long surface.

What this demonstrates

The concept can expand beyond a logo into a complete campaign layout, while keeping the same visual rhythm.

Concept mock-up; shown to demonstrate the proposed design, not a completed installation.

12 / FACADE BANNER

Adapt to a narrow format.

What I designed

A vertical banner that brings together the portrait imagery and the stacked championship mark.

Why this layout

The tall format needs a simple hierarchy. The photograph draws attention from above, while the logo sits clearly in the lighter lower section.

What this demonstrates

The identity can change proportions without losing its character. The stacked lock-up is especially useful when width is limited.

13 / STREET DISPLAY

Tell the story in one poster.

What I designed

A street display using preparation imagery, a deep background, red panels and the championship logo.

Why this layout

The image establishes the subject first. The red panel gives the supporting message and event details a clear place to sit.

What this demonstrates

A compact public-facing layout can carry both cultural atmosphere and practical information within the same design system.

14 / WRISTBANDS

Keep the identity clear at a smaller scale.

What I designed

Wristband variations using the logo, repeated lettering and the main palette.

Why this layout

A wristband has very little space. Repeating simple brand elements keeps it recognisable without relying on a full poster composition.

What this demonstrates

The visual system can work on a small wearable surface as well as large event graphics.

Concept mock-up / event wristbands. No access categories are claimed.

15 / INTERIOR BANNERS

Bring the visual world into the venue.

What I designed

A group of hanging banners using different parts of the identity: photography, the logo, red panels and fine line graphics.

Why this layout

Each banner has a clear role. Seen together, they create variety while repeating the same colour and shape language.

What this demonstrates

The concept can shape an event environment, connecting individual graphics into one recognisable experience.

Concept mock-up / venue graphics.

16 / MOTION DIRECTION

Motion starts with the same ritual.

The idea

I used a sequence of simple drawings to lead into the logo. The animation introduces the tools and gestures of gahwa before showing the event name.

The visual treatment

Fine red-brown linework sits on a light paper-like background. The restrained palette connects the video to the graphic identity.

The purpose

The sequence gives the logo a story. Viewers see a connection to the practice before the final mark appears.

Frames taken from your supplied 1920 x 1080 animation.
